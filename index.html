New to crypto? Start here.

This is a normal Visa card. Three things are worth knowing before you sign up, and none of them require any background in crypto.

  1. You load it, then you spend it.

    Add money to your account and the card draws on it. Nothing is spent that you did not put there.

  2. You keep control of the money.

    Your balance sits in a wallet only you can move. That is what "non-custodial" means: no company holds your funds on your behalf.

  3. It earns while it waits.

    Money you have not spent yet sits in a vault and earns a published rate. Rates are variable and not guaranteed.

Straight answers

The questions that actually matter.

I have never used crypto. Is this for me?
You do not need to know anything about crypto to use the card. You open an account, verify your identity, add money, and spend. The one idea worth understanding is that your money is held in a wallet you control rather than on a company's balance sheet — which is also why you can earn on it while you are not spending. See Start here above for the plain-English version.
Is opening an account really free?
Yes. Core, the entry level, is listed as free for everyone on ether.fi's comparison page, with no annual fee and one free virtual card. A physical card is $50 on Core. Higher levels come from membership points, a vault deposit or staked ETHFI — or you can buy the level for a year at $199 or $999. All benefits are subject to eligibility and limits.
How does the 3% cashback actually work?
The rate steps down as your monthly spend rises. At Core: 3% on the first $2,000, then 1% on the next $1,000, then 0.5% with no cap. Higher tiers extend the top band — Luxe to $10,000, Pinnacle to $50,000. Cashback is credited in ETHFI, not cash, and locks for 7 days after a transaction clears, claimable from $5. Rates are variable and can change.
What does it cost, and what about foreign currency?
Nothing annual at Core, and one free virtual card; a physical card is $50. On currency conversion, ether.fi publishes an FX base rate of about 1% plus an added margin of 0–0.5% at Core. Euro purchases are listed at a 0% FX markup, currently in beta — and because the FX benefit is separate, the EUR cashback rate is lower: 3% down to 0.1% as spend rises at Core.
Do I have to sell my crypto to spend it?
No, and that is the point. In Direct mode your purchase is deducted from the USDC or LiquidUSD in your account. In Borrow mode you spend against the rest of your portfolio and repay whenever you like — ether.fi states there is no repayment schedule. Be clear what Borrow mode is: a loan against collateral, with interest, and liquidatable if your collateral value falls far enough.
Is my money insured?
Not as a deposit. ether.fi states it is not a regulated financial institution and that holdings are not bank deposits and are not FDIC, SIPC or otherwise insured. Separately, ether.fi runs a Cash Protection Program that may reimburse losses from unauthorised activity in covered smart contracts: $10,000 at Core, rising to $500,000 at VIP, with a $5 million programme cap. It excludes market losses, de-pegs, oracle failures and compromised credentials or devices, and a self-submitted claim carries a $100 fee. Do not read it as deposit insurance.
Is that iPhone 18 promotion real?
Yes, and it is time-limited. One in every ten qualifying pre-orders receives 100% cashback up to $2,000. The window is 12–18 September 2026, entries are final 30 September, and rewards are paid 1 November 2026. Purchases must be $1,000 or more in a single transaction from Apple. It is a random draw, capped at 500 qualifying pre-orders or $100,000. Core members need one qualifying referral to enter. Full terms are linked in the footer — read them before relying on this.
Where can I not use it?
Availability depends on your country and, in the US, your state, and ether.fi publishes the lists in its help centre. Card spending can also fail in further countries because of sanctions or payment-network rules. Because these lists change, check ether.fi's own availability page before you sign up rather than relying on any third-party summary — including this one.
What are the real risks?
Smart-contract risk, slashing risk on staked ETH, liquidity and redemption delays, liquidation risk on borrowed balances, and yields that are variable rather than promised. Two incidents are on ether.fi's own public log. In September 2024 an attempted domain-account takeover at its registrar was locked down; ether.fi reported all funds safe. On 11 September 2026 a missing authorisation check in a legacy withdrawal helper (a contract Liquid vaults had stopped using about a year earlier) let an attacker move assets from wallets that still held an old approval — ether.fi puts the realised user loss at about $40,000 on Ethereum plus roughly 2,257 sETHFI on Arbitrum, and states all affected users are being reimbursed in full. Audited code can still be exploited; ether.fi's own documentation says audits reduce risk, not remove it.
